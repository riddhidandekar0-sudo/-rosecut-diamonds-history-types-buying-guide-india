## What is a Rosecut Diamond

A rosecut diamond is one of the oldest diamond
cutting styles in the world. Unlike modern brilliant
cut diamonds that are designed for maximum sparkle
and fire, rosecut diamonds are known for their
soft, subtle, romantic glow.

**Key characteristics:**
- Flat base with no pavilion (bottom)
- Domed top with triangular facets
- Anywhere from 3 to 24 facets
- Lower profile than modern cuts
- Soft, diffused light reflection

The name comes from the resemblance of the facet
pattern to the petals of an opening rose bud —
layered, symmetrical, and naturally beautiful.

## History of Rosecut Diamonds

The rosecut diamond has a history spanning nearly
500 years, making it one of the most storied
diamond cuts in existence.

### Timeline

| Period | Development |
|--------|-------------|
| 1500s | Originated in India and the Middle East |
| 1600s | Popularised across Europe by Dutch cutters |
| 1700s | Became the dominant diamond cut worldwide |
| 1800s | Peak popularity during Victorian and Georgian eras |
| 1900s | Declined as brilliant cut technology emerged |
| 2000s | Strong revival in vintage and luxury jewellery |
| 2020s | One of the most sought-after cuts in India |

India has a deep and personal connection with
rosecut diamonds. Long before modern cutting
technology existed, Indian artisans were shaping
diamonds into rosecut forms by hand — a tradition
that continues in fine jewellery studios
across Mumbai and Surat even today.

The Victorian and Edwardian eras saw rosecut
diamonds at their most celebrated — set into
gold and platinum pieces with intricate milgrain
borders and nature-inspired motifs that are
still widely admired and referenced today.

## How Rosecut Differs from Brilliant Cut

Understanding the difference between a rosecut
and a modern round brilliant cut is essential
before making a buying decision.

| Feature | Rosecut Diamond | Brilliant Cut Diamond |
|---------|-----------------|----------------------|
| Facets | 3 to 24 | 57 to 58 |
| Base | Flat | Pointed pavilion |
| Profile | Low dome | Tall and deep |
| Light | Soft, diffused glow | High sparkle and fire |
| Origin | 16th century | 20th century |
| Feel | Vintage, romantic | Modern, brilliant |
| Setting style | Bezel, prong, pavé | Prong, halo, channel |
| Visibility of inclusions | More visible | Less visible |
| Price | Generally lower | Generally higher |

Neither cut is superior — they serve different
aesthetics and suit different personalities.
Rosecut diamonds suit those who prefer understated
elegance over flashy brilliance.

## Types of Rosecut Diamonds

Not all rosecut diamonds are the same. There are
several variations, each with its own character
and appeal.

### 1. Single Rosecut
- 3 to 8 facets
- Simplest and most ancient form
- Very flat with minimal faceting
- Best for small accent stones

### 2. Double Rosecut
- Facets on both top and bottom
- More symmetrical than single cut
- Greater light play than single rosecut
- Rarely seen in modern jewellery

### 3. Dutch Rosecut
- 24 facets arranged in two rows
- Most classic and refined rosecut form
- Associated with 17th century European jewellery
- High collector value

### 4. Briolette Rosecut
- Fully faceted teardrop shape
- No flat base — facets cover entire surface
- Often used as drops in earrings and pendants
- Extremely elegant and feminine

### 5. Half Dutch Rosecut
- Flat back with 12 facets on top
- Common in antique Indian jewellery
- Widely used in traditional kundan and polki work

### 6. Antique Rosecut
- Irregular, hand-cut facets
- Each stone slightly unique
- Found in heirloom and estate jewellery
- Highly valued by collectors

## The 4Cs for Rosecut Diamonds

The standard 4Cs — Cut, Colour, Clarity, and Carat —
apply to rosecut diamonds but with some important
differences compared to modern brilliant cuts.

### Cut
For rosecut diamonds, cut quality refers to
the symmetry of the dome and the precision of
the facet arrangement. A well-cut rosecut will
have an even, balanced dome and facets that
meet cleanly at the apex.

### Colour
Colour is more visible in rosecut diamonds than
in brilliant cuts because the flat base allows
light to pass through rather than reflect back.
This means:
- Lower colour grades are more noticeable
- D to G colour is recommended for white rosecuts
- Fancy colours (yellow, grey, brown) look
  exceptionally beautiful in rosecut form

### Clarity
Because rosecut diamonds have fewer facets and
a flat base, inclusions are more visible to
the naked eye. However, this is not always
a disadvantage — slight inclusions give
antique rosecuts a natural, organic character.

Recommended clarity: VS1 to SI1 for clean
appearance. SI2 and below may show visible
inclusions depending on stone size.

### Carat
Rosecut diamonds appear larger than brilliant
cut diamonds of the same carat weight because
they are cut shallow and wide. This makes them
excellent value — you get more visible surface
area for the same weight.

> A 1 carat rosecut diamond can appear as large
> as a 1.5 carat brilliant cut diamond of the
> same diameter.

## Why Rosecut is Popular in India

Rosecut diamonds hold a uniquely special place
in Indian jewellery culture for several reasons.

**Historical connection**
India was one of the original sources of rosecut
diamonds and the tradition of hand-finishing
diamond surfaces in a rosecut style predates
European adoption by centuries.

**Polki and Kundan tradition**
Indian polki jewellery — one of the most revered
forms of traditional Indian jewellery — uses
unfinished or minimally finished rosecut diamonds
set in gold with foil backing. This tradition
dates back to the Mughal era and continues
to be popular for bridal jewellery today.

**Romantic aesthetic**
The soft glow of a rosecut diamond pairs
beautifully with the rich colours, embroidery,
and silk of traditional Indian clothing. It
complements rather than competes.

**Value**
Rosecut diamonds are generally priced lower
than brilliant cuts of the same carat weight,
making luxury jewellery more accessible while
retaining genuine diamond quality.

**Revival of vintage aesthetics**
India's urban luxury jewellery buyers are
increasingly drawn to pieces with history,
character, and individuality — qualities
that rosecut diamonds naturally embody.

## Best Jewellery Pieces Using Rosecut Diamonds

Rosecut diamonds work beautifully across a wide
range of jewellery types.

### Rings
Rosecut diamonds in rings offer a low-profile,
elegant look that is comfortable for everyday wear.
Popular settings include:

- Bezel set solitaire — clean and modern
- Vintage prong with milgrain border — classic
- Halo of smaller rosecuts — romantic and full
- East-west elongated setting — contemporary

### Earrings
The flat base of rosecut diamonds makes them
ideal for earrings where the stone lies close
to the ear. Popular styles:

- Stud earrings in bezel settings
- Drop earrings with rosecut centre stones
- Chandelier earrings mixing rosecuts with
  coloured gemstones

### Necklaces and Pendants
Rosecut diamonds in pendants catch light
beautifully when worn against skin. Popular forms:

- Single rosecut solitaire pendant
- Cluster pendants mixing rosecuts with emeralds
  or rubies
- Art Deco inspired geometric pendant designs

### Bracelets
Rosecut diamonds line bracelets gracefully —
their flat profile sits flush and comfortable
on the wrist. Popular styles:

- Tennis bracelets with rosecut diamonds
- Mixed gemstone bracelets alternating rosecuts
  with emeralds or sapphires
- Stacking bracelets for layered looks
